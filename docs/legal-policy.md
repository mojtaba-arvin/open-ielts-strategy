# Legal, copyright, and content rights policy

**This document is a conservative project policy, not legal advice.** Copyright, database rights, contract terms, and exceptions are fact-sensitive. Obtain qualified legal review before publishing third-party material or relying on a statutory exception.

## Separate rights layers
Assess independently: copyright in recordings/transcripts/articles/test questions/model answers; database rights; trademarks; contractual access/use terms; privacy/data protection; and rights in project code, skill text, and original evaluation content.

A public URL, attribution, search indexing, or download availability does not establish permission to copy, transform, publish, or redistribute.

## Project default
The public repository must not contain third-party transcripts, audio/video, official IELTS questions, paid preparation materials, answer keys, or substantial excerpts unless the exact intended use is approved and documented. Initial fixtures are original or synthetic. Private study, analysis, publication, redistribution, and commercial use are not interchangeable.

## All Ears English
The publisher's terms/policies have stated personal-use limitations, restrictions around business/commercial use, and ownership claims over podcast content. Treat transcripts and recordings as restricted unless current terms and a documented legal assessment establish a specific permitted operation. Do not publish episode-derived transcripts or substantial excerpts by default.

A statutory text-and-data-mining exception is a separate legal question. Contractual terms do not automatically resolve every statutory issue, and an exception does not automatically authorize public redistribution of retained copies. Review current terms, jurisdiction, lawful access, reservations, and the exact workflow with counsel.

## IELTS materials and marks
IELTS websites and materials may be owned or licensed by IELTS Partners and third parties. Do not copy official questions, sample answers, examiner materials, logos, or other content into public fixtures without written permission or a documented legal basis. IELTS is a protected trademark; use its name only as needed to describe subject matter/compatibility, avoid branding that implies endorsement, and retain a clear non-affiliation notice.

## EU and Germany: text and data mining
Directive (EU) 2019/790 distinguishes Article 3 (qualifying research organisations/cultural heritage institutions conducting scientific research with lawful access) from Article 4 (broader TDM of lawfully accessible works, subject to rightsholder reservation; online reservations must be expressed appropriately, including machine-readable means).

German UrhG §44b includes lawful-access, retention/deletion, and rights-reservation conditions. Applicability depends on actor, purpose, source, access, reservation, and retention. Do not assume an exception permits publishing source copies or reconstructed transcripts in a public repository.

DSM Directive Article 7(1) addresses contractual provisions contrary to Articles 3 and 4. Terms should not be treated as automatically overriding those statutory exceptions; this does not make downstream publication or redistribution automatically lawful.

## United Kingdom
CDPA 1988 §29A is limited to computational analysis for non-commercial research by a person with lawful access, with sufficient acknowledgement where required. Copies made under the exception must not be transferred or used for other purposes absent authorization. It is not a general license for public or commercial transcript publication.

## United States
Fair use under 17 U.S.C. §107 is a case-by-case four-factor analysis: purpose/character, nature of the work, amount/substantiality, and market effect. There is no universal safe percentage or word count. Attribution, educational purpose, transformation, or non-commercial intent do not automatically establish fair use.

## Database rights and privacy
A qualifying database may attract separate protection (including German UrhG §§87a et seq.); repeated/systematic extraction can raise issues even where individual facts are not protected by copyright. For learner submissions or personal data, apply data minimisation, purpose limitation, storage limitation, security, and an appropriate lawful basis. Do not publish personal submissions as evaluation data without separate review and a valid basis.

## License separation
- `LICENSE`: MIT applies to project software code only.
- `LICENSE-CONTENT`: original skill and editorial content is separately reserved unless explicitly licensed per file.
- Third-party materials retain their owners' rights; this project grants no rights in them.

A future open license for skill content must be selected explicitly.

## Release gate
Block release if included third-party material is `pending`, `under_review`, `restricted`, `blocked`, or `expired`; permission evidence is missing; the intended operation exceeds the approved scope; or legal uncertainty remains. Automation validates metadata, not legal conclusions.
