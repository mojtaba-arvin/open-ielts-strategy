# Source ingestion and provenance

## Default
The public repository is metadata-only for third-party sources. Do not store transcripts, audio, video, scraped article bodies, official test questions, answer keys, or substantial excerpts.

## Workflow
1. Identify exact source, owner/licensor, URL, retrieval date, and material type.
2. Review current terms and copyright notices; preserve dated evidence privately.
3. Distinguish access, analysis, extraction, transformation, quotation, publication, redistribution, commercial use, and derivative works.
4. Assess applicable law by territory and purpose; an exception in one jurisdiction is not worldwide clearance.
5. Record allowed/prohibited uses, attribution, modification, redistribution, commercial scope, territory, and expiry.
6. Store evidence outside Git and reference it using a private identifier.
7. Set a registry status. `cleared` and `licensed` apply only to a narrowly specified use, never blanket permission.
8. Keep source content out of public fixtures; prefer independently authored or synthetic examples.
9. Recheck terms and reservations before releases or when notified of changes.

## Statuses
- `pending`: not reviewed.
- `under_review`: review underway or legal interpretation uncertain.
- `cleared`: reviewer approved a narrowly specified use based on a documented legal basis; not a general license.
- `licensed`: written permission covers the exact use and territory.
- `restricted`: some uses may be allowed, but publication/redistribution is not cleared.
- `blocked`: use is not approved.
- `expired`: permission or clearance expired or is no longer reliable.

## Permission record
Capture private evidence reference, reviewer, review date, rights holder, allowed use, commercial status, redistribution, modification, attribution, territory, expiry, and conditions. Never publish contracts, private correspondence, contact details, or restricted copies in Git.
